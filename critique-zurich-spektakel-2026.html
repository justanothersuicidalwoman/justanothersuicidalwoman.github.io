Zürcher Theater Spektakel – careless critique

Writing about everything I witnessed between the 20th and 30th of August at the Zürcher Theater Spektakel will not be an easy feat, but we were all born to try! The title offers a good disclaimer here: this critique might be a bit careless!

Only on 1st of September now I find the time and energy to sit down and write my reflection. As part of the what and talk cohort alongside eight other artists, I was invited to the festival to watch performances and reflect on them primarily on the group level, and occasionally with the curatorial team. Attending a festival (almost) in its entirety, with (almost) nothing to do but observe and debate, was my dream setup. What follows is a personal account touching on upon the performance I watched, the experience of watch and talk and what lingered in my memory.

Every journey begins with departure; a knot in the stomach and the quiet panic of forgetting something essential. This time, I also carry a specific anxiety: what if my surgical scar opens again? One month before the departure day, I had a small surgery on my back. Right after the recovery period and stich removal, the wound ruptured. In panic, I got stiches back and an extra month of rest. The trauma has not left me yet; I have a fear to lift my suitcase. T wakes up to bring my suitcase down for me. Half naked, he gives me a kiss, one that I don't expect.

On the train to Schiphol, I sit next to a woman traveling with heavy luggage. We start a conversation to double-check if we were on the right line. She tells me she lives in Johannesburg and had been visiting her daughter in The Hague. She tells me how safe she felt in this city, and how in Johannesburg, she is always in fear; she cannot take a walk anywhere, and life outside is reduced to driving between gated malls and private property. She claims there is no buses there, no trains, or even postal system left in Johannesburg, insisting it had all been destroyed by Black people. She lived in perpetual terror of them.

I really don't know what to say. It is too much inherited racism in her. I also refrain from asking her why she does not move from there, how can bring up the dislocation and colonization of centuries ago in the remaining 10 minutes of this train ride encounter? I stay quiet. She asks about my travel. If I am scared travelling alone. She even fears for me. She fears for her daughter that wants to go to Berlin but she has told her Berlin is too dangerous. I tell her "your daughter is a grown up independent woman living abroad, she can survive Berlin or any other city. (something I hope someone tells my own mother) we part in the airport, I wish her a safe travel.

In the airport I lean on chivalry of strangers; I ask for help, from everyone that was closer, whenever I needed to lift my suitcase I asked a man to do it: the young man before the security, the cute man after, the grumpy shuttle bus driver, the kind man at the airstairs who carried my luggage all the way up to the flight attendant. When she hears the situation, she stowed it in the crew locker and promised to request assistance upon our arrival in Switzerland!

My streak of good luck seemed complete when I find my seat at the emergency exit with premium legroom without paying for it. Just settling in, the flight attendant comes to give the safety briefing, then she sees me and relocate me because the person sitting there must be in peak physical condition. Oh well. I am moved to the back sit next to an older lady. She start talking and asking questions. I always want to be polite and engage in conversations but her pungent breath makes it unbearable. It makes me want to stop talking to her but because she continues for a while, I have to look dead ahead while answering her questions.

After a bit of this difficult conversation, we arrive to Zurich. I ask the attendant to give me my bag, she kindly takes it out for me and shows me to the ground staff. The escort immediately unfolds a wheelchair and gestures me to sit. I froze in shock! What started as s subtle request for help is unfolding in a wheelchair. "I don't want it." I tell him. "I just need someone to carry my bag down. I can walk". After hearing this, he refuses to help at all. Since there are no stairs here. I find my own way out.

I have no cellular connection, I look for the promised airport pickup with the sign the Zürcher Theater Spektakel. I don't see this person. Hot, tired, and irritated, my romanticized image of a welcoming host disappears. Had they forgotten me? After half an hour, a woman rushed in the airport with the sign, she apologizes for a car trouble. I drop the frustration right away: 1.she is a fellow woman. 2. I don't want to start the trip with a bad mood. 3.they did not forget me at the end!

We talk on the car ride. She is a playwriter and tells me about her play: a father and a daughter that don't get along, go on a trip to Serbia to find out traces of their ancestral roots. It is the her story and her father's, she did this research in Serbia with him, about their ancestral minority . To find things about it, and about him, and eventually about herself. How she concludes the subject in one sentence stays with me: "I went to look for things, I was trying to find what is all the restrictions about, but at the end I didn't find anything to be proud of." That's too real to be true.

We arrive at the guesthouse that I will be my home coming 10 days. I am guided to my room and it is a beautiful one. I have half an hour to go downstairs for the introduction; it's tight! I just brush my teeth, wash my face, change clothes and head downstairs.

The cohort is here. We were handed per diem and our artist passes that will open all the doors! We head out to the festival ground, which is a bit far from our lodgings.

I am amazed by the scenery, when we arrive. Festival is right at the shore of Lake Zurich is magical. The drinking water tastes so crisp that the curatorial team welcomes us with fresh glasses of it. After round of introduction, we ask about how they made the selection of watch and talk 2026: each one of us has been recommended by someone as a promising emerging artist.

DAY 1

The schedule is launched immediately: We are going to watch three shows today almost back to back. First up is Kafan by Palestinian artist Aws Alzubaidy. The piece starts with a citation from Italian write Primo Levi: "After Gaza, it is time to acknowledge that the attempt to humanize history has failed – and that there will be no second attempt."

Aws's work is a hand full of grief: he shows videos from three years ago almost in the same stage setup where he announces casualties of the genocide, and reenact it by enormously bigger numbers of today's. He scatters stones across the stage as graves, or houses crushed under armor. A remote-control toy tank (terrifying in its mechanical precision), moves in the room while Aws dances extremely between morning and frustration.

He wraps a skeleton rested on a moving table, in Kafan: the traditional white shroud used across west Asia to prepare the dead for burial. Yet the piece is scattered: There are too many things that are said or shown that don't land anywhere, they are abruptly abandoned. There are many signs that we don't have any tool to read them, and despite being aggressive in movement, this work stays very inward/isolated/insular; like a personal collage. It is extrovert in delivery but very subjective in legibility.

There are two sexual associations that both goes wrong in my opinion and I rather look away in those moments: first Aws wears a miniskirt and a tank top and symbolically mounts the tank. In the other, when he is talking in a microphone, he occasionally move it in the direction of his back. Rather than deepening on the violence and vulnerability, these two movements are equally encodable but more disturbing due to what they are.

The second performance is Galactic crush 2: Into the Cold by Stephanie Kayal and Abed Kobeissy. A sci-fi story about war in Lebanon, where two characters flee their homeland to a galactic journey in their spaceship. Through a unique, exaggerated, absurd lens, Galactic crush encapsulates the very core feeling of displacement, abandoning home to escape hum of hunting drowns overhead. It captures the heavy aftermath of that flight as well; the meaninglessness, the survivor's guilt, the profound detachment, the extreme boredom of endless drifting, the non-belonging and just getting further from self. Galactic Crush was a stroke on my painful broken heart; a fantastical perspective of what I carry around after leaving home. Although the work occasionally steps in and out from its own world to explain the first performance or to explain some real moments of making of this duo piece while one of them is on in Lebanon one abroad, it maintains a cohesive aesthetics, choreography, text and its delivery in a wholeness that I thoroughly admire.

The next piece is called Sorry, it was meant to be funny by Malice Ghazzale. From the moment I see the performer she looks angry; That is part of the aesthetic. dressed in heavy military boots, and a T-shirt that says: "Bark for me", her backdrop projection is chaotic, thrashy content and images. Straddling between performance, stand-up comedy and lecture performance, the description frames the work as a stand-up tragedy; In being so, it keeps the audience on their feet by barrage of harrowing global facts, specifically about war, and grotesque humor, like jokes about dead babies… It is necessary to see angry people and better, angry women on stage. It makes sense to be angry at this time, to me the performance feels like a sudden outburst of angry, like a sneeze, it resealed, but it doesn't land, once the air is cleared, there is not much residue left. When it ended, I simply needed a breath.

The day has been just too long, its weigh in settling in me. But still I can't keep my promise to myself to go home directly and rest. I am too curious for this! I mingle around Have a drink!

DAY 2

Second day we have our first reflection session. Our conversation is naturally centered on the fact that every piece we watched dealt explicitly with war and genocide. Some people find it "too late", some people find it "anyway better than silence". We also discuss the dynamic of representing other identity in an opulent city of Zurich.

During these days, our group has moments of practice sharing, so we get to know each other better. The first person is me; I read a text detailing my personal journey into writing and try out an exercise after telling bits of Persian history and philosophy: if we were to save the language we speak now, only by holding onto a letter each, what that letter would be, and what words matter to us to save?

When I tried this exercise at home, I thought about words like freedom, love, rave … but the group took a far more grounded approach: starting with the letter E , they began crafting fundamental words like Egg. Once everyone had their letter, I introduced another aspect of my practice: in how I make my writing visceral, I asked them to write their letters on their tongues with the edible ink that I brought from home. It was an icebreaking moment!

The fourth performance we watch is called KIZAZI by Congolese artist Stephanie Mwamba. A solo dance piece on the topic of sexual violence against women. Stephanie's physical command is impressive; the way she uses shapes and shades makes a new image of the body that evolves to into womanhood. KIZAZI means generation, posterity, and I also recall the word womb from a conversation about this piece. The woman is struggling or participating in a ritual. Although the symbolism behind the ritual, with water and flowers and a circle, stays unsolved to me but I get the feeling of in-between, suffering but participating. At the end of the piece there is a fast flash of projection of the written fact. A fragment of explanation regarding women's life in Democratic Republic of Congo.

Next performance is called Machito by Martin Soria. Another solo dance piece. While we queued for ticket scanning, the performer moves into the crowd with an exaggerative traditional Argentina folk costume, stamping heavy boots bells to the ground. The stamping intensifies as we enter the theatre and take a sit. Silently, Martin recruits men from the audience to help strip off the macho costume. Slowly undressing, Martin changes into an exaggerative female version of the traditional costume; tiny skirt and top. Although the physical transformation might be personally liberating for the performer, to me it remains trapped in a strict gender binary. Since the costumes and movements are too clear or too literal, the transition is not emancipating from the gender norms. It is like a shortcut to womanhood, following the logic of a cliché that frustrates women.

At the end of this day, artists and programmers gather in Zentral (festival's central lounge, somewhere to drink tea or beer, and get massage!) for a networking event. Although I tend to fear any networking opportunity, I make myself attend this one because I HAVE/WANT/PROMISED to make the most of my time here and do my best to sustain my practice as an artist. I mostly end up talking to people who I already know and already tried my chance with… except for K. I meet K in this event, who I will spend some of my free time with, in the coming week.

DAY 3

The third day began with a collective artist lunch, which I missed. Later, our group gets into the studio for another practice sharing and reflection session. We revisited the themes of past two days: identity, representing and who gets granted a platform to speak, and whose interests do international festivals serve?

The first show of the third day, the 6th show overall, is called Hallow embraces by Ramz Slam and Nowwar Salem. The piece itself does not connect with me. The artists themselves certainly do. The following day, we have an intimate Q&A with the duo. Ramz (what a beautiful name) is based in Palestine and Nowwar is based in Europe (I don't remember where), talking to them is far more grounded and solid than the work. In person, their artistic intentions are more clear. The performance at some moments is very representative (such as imitating the movement of digging) and at some moments is very insular in its poetics, making little effort to connect to the audience. The makers acknowledge this, mentioning during making process they shielded the project from any outside eye because they believe that would make them doubt their own intuitions and desires.

The second performance of the day is BOTO; story of a birth Fred Raposo. I have watched this work several times, at this point is hard for me to write about it but I will try. The piece is structurally tight and effortlessly engages mostly by the charismatic and funny characters of Fred and Robin. It weaves together personal history of looking for biological parents and colonial violence.

He third and final show of the day is AAA by Jana Jacuka. A skillful research on possibilities and limits of human voice. In this performance, Jana is relearning the language or making the very simple statements of "I am here" "you are here", through yodeling. Some parts of the performance pushes the body toward hyper exhaustion through extreme breathwork and relentless movements. Jana's examination of physical and vocal capacity is undeniable, but in conceptually it is slightly uneven; What begins as a powerful, grounded investigation into presence and survival, gradually shifts toward broader apocalyptic imagery (phrases like "the ceiling collapses" or "the floor collapses") without grounding those metaphors.

DAY 4

The fourth day, as I mentioned, opened with the artist talk with the makers of Hallow Embraces. I like to write here one quote from that exchange:

Audience: is joy resistance?

Artist: no. joy is a coping mechanism.

Questioner: is art resistance?

Artist: no. art is important, but not resistance. Sacrifice is what make resistance. If there is no sacrifice, there is no resistance. It costs anyone anything.

After the conversation, we have another reflection session. And following that, another peer-sharing, which I unfortunately missed because of a sudden family emergency… I spend the hour outside, crying on the phone, thinking of the catastrophe that might happen… for now, everybody proved to be safe, but I had no mental capacity to join the group. I wait outside, better not to take so much attention with red eyes in other's sharing.

The only performance we watch on this day is BREL by Anne Teresa De Keermaeker and Solal Mariotte. In takes place in a stage called SEEBUHNE; a stunning temporary stage built on a little island in lake Zurich. The city's ritual of constructing all these massive venues only to dismantle them weeks later is remarkable. With the stage hovering on the water, ships moving in the back drop and the ambient of the waves, are new charm I experience in the theatre.

BREL is a tribute to Belgian singer and song writer Jacque Brel. Unfamiliar with the French lyrics and unable to read the German surtitle, I am happy I smuggled in a bottle of wine, and took a seat all the way in the back. The performance feels like a live music video, or a bodily dialogue with one's beloved songs. There is an undeniable pleasure to move to the music that one adores but that pleasure does not automatically translates into an engaging movement for the audience. The work could potentially take things further and integorate concepts of reenactment or cross mediums interpretation, but to me it drowned in itself by repetition.

DAY 5

The fifth day began with two practice sharing followed by a groep reflection session. Then we headed to the 10th show of this journey: Trapped in Hope by Palestinian artist Nabil Al-Rabaee alongside Spanish artists Miguel Oyarzun and Juan Ayala.

The Performance starts with text projected on the screen: "imagine a man... Imagine an Arab man…. Do you see home with camels?...." from these first lines, I get an immediate skepticism regarding how the piece positions itself toward a predominantly white audience. Soon the actor enters, an Arab man as promised. Approaching the audience, members, he spreads his arms, silently prompting them to frisk him. Some people do, some freeze, some spread their arms to imitate him. Expressing disappointment, he moves down to the stage and invites a person to search him thoroughly. A member of our cohort. She awkwardly refuses, but he insists, for moments that take like minutes. She obviously doesn't want to do that, but she eventually does so anyway so the show can go on…

Trapped in Hope is framed as a documentary theatre, centers on Nabil, the director of Freedom Theatre in Jenin, who was born and raised in a West Bank refugee camp. The performance unfolds as a collection of personal memories, through different forms of reenactment. Almost following documentary mechanics of storytelling, it skillfully manipulates the audience's emotions, bridging the distance between stage and stalls both physically and mentally, through targeted guilt, empathy and feeling of unusefulness.

The distance between the performer and the audience start to fade; The performer brings a person to act on the stage. He directs her to say a line: "I am ready to join the fight." Guiding her how to say the line with a strength and calm. When they repeat it for three times, just before the final take, another woman from the audience in the third row, jumps and says the line, hands pinned to her body like a soldier: "I am ready to join the fight."

Theatre can be scary. Theatre is such a potent tool for activating, for provoking, for propaganda. Theatre can absorb bodies in itself. Trapped in hope is a didactic piece about the genocide in Palestine, but the tools that it uses, the proximity it has with its audience. makes me uneasy. The adroit storyteller can make people do things that I am not sure if they would do so if there was more critical distance.

Despite the depiction of various relentless images, I wonder how nobody leaves the theatre.

In the last part of the performance, the performer asked a German speaking woman (why he keeps using only women?) to translate his story for the audience, the woman sits in the middle of the stage and Nabil tells the story in her ear and she tells it back imitating the feeling and tone: a deeply moving childhood memory, about dressing up as a super hero to impress a girl, but being cornered, humiliated threatened by Israeli soldiers. But the use of verbatim technique feels troubling: verbatim mostly is used to protect the identity of the original speaker, if the speaker is Nabil and he is telling it right in her ear, what is the emotional and the proximity of the narrative doing with the audience?

In the closing moment, Nabil dropped a disorienting bomb, he says that is mainly an actor, and all he has told us might be lies, that we have to look for the truth ourselves. This is followed then by the most enthusiastic applaud I witness in the festival. Why do we crave being manipulated? Why do we surrender to emotional orchestration? Why did no one leave? And why did no one challenge his final admission that it all might have been a lie?

We have to rush to the next event: a concert by Eartheater (apparently earth eater not ear theater). I don't know this singer but I hear days before from friends that she is praised. Needing a palate cleanser, I ask K to grab me a cocktail. Rosemary sour. We head to the SEEBUHNE for the concert.

It is nice. Sitting by the water, her ethereal vocal range evokes an experimental Lana Del Rey. I enjoy the music and have been listening to 2 songs from her since that day. The only downside is the light design for the concert, I have shades to wear but still it is too much is in the eye.

DAY 6

The next day, we, the participants of watch and talk have a 10 minutes window to present our artistic practice to the curators of the festival. I am the first person and got some kind of flu, or I am just allergic to this situations. I am always uncomfortable around authority; My dad, state security, programmers. I guess I get anxious and then lose interest as a coping mechanism. a bad habit I desperately need to break.

I present my work, as did others. It is funny to see how different we are in this situation. But at the end, except one question that I get from generous and sensitive Marta Keil, I wonder if the words said in this session are lost in the uproar of the festival? I hope not.

The performance we watch on the sixth day, the 12th overall is called Dans La mesure de l'impossible ( within the limits of the impossible) by Tiago Rodrigues. The piece subjects individuals who dedicate themselves to humanitarian aid. A vast white fabric covers the stage, suspended by ropes to perimeter hooks, transforming the material into an abstract landscape of emergency tents and rugged mountain ranges.

Four actors come in. two men and two women. The performers address the audience as the makers of the piece, interviewing them. this way, we are continually reminded of the theatrical medium itself, cleverly, we are the makers of the performance, who will decide how to tell these stories later.

The stories are about the impossible as a place, a war zone, or a catastrophe site. They visualize brutal images, unvarnished accounts of death, human suffering, and the psychological rupture of returning to everyday life. People who their stories will ruin the any vibe, people who find themselves in the middle of hell asking "where the fuck am I?".

Although the show follows conventional theatre, and we can ask important questions like Does theatre do something for this subject or just uses it? Or what is the outcome of framing other's misery? Or why the performance chooses to be artistically so clean and beautiful? precisely because of the critical distance built into the structure, the reality of the subject matter lands with devastating rawness and clarity. Unlike previous performance, this piece has walk-outs.

DAY 7

On the seventh day, we take our adventure further than festival ground. The planners intentionally built this excursion day into our agenda so we explore other theatre venues and performance spaces in Zurich, so Theater Spektakel didn't hold a monopoly on what we know of theatre in Zurich! Their institutional self-awareness is refreshing.

First stop is Gessneralle, where we meet one of the co-directors and she explains how their focus right now is on the accessibly. I find it fascinating that they are making the space inviting for diverse physical and neurodivergent needs. Where we sit to talk, happen to be a deaf artist taking a break nearby. an interpreter immediately began signing our group discussion so the artist could participate if they chose. The venue is fully wheelchair accessible, with adaptable seating where standard chairs can be swapped for beanbags. They even have a zero-COVID safety zone, accessible only with a negative test or an N95 mask.

Then we visited Tanzhuaus Zurich, a dedicated dance space for local artist. Very kind people, very beautiful building.

Afterward, we treated ourselves to a refreshing Swiss experience! jumping in to the river, going with the current, grabbing the exit bars to haul ourselves out, and doing back up to do this again.

Final site visit was Zirkus Quartier, an inspiring contemporary circus. Many kids around having classes at the time we visit, the place was buzzing with energy.

After a quick dinner and almost impossible conversation because of the noise in Zum Gual (a grill restaurant under bridge) we headed to the first and last performance of the day: Honda Romance by Vimola Pons at the massive Schiffbau Halle.

As the lead actress says, the theatre starts from its end, when the Honda satellite has crashed onto the stage, pinning the protagonist beneath its wreckage while onlookers film the incident on their phones instead of helping. Then in the next chapter, the satellite lifts up, the actress trapped in the middle of three air canons. This chapter is a a long monologue that lost me completely, drifting from topic to topic, trying to be witty but it does not land and is flat for me (maybe because it is in French and I have to follow it in the subtitle, but I am doing my best so I doubt if it really makes a point, but for sure it dilutes the rhythm.)

The third and longest chapter is like a fashion runway, awkwardly transported on a traditional proscenium stage. Performers cycle through rapid-fire changes of costumes, personas, and moods, one after another or in groups. The visuals are so hyper-saturated and open-ended that it drifts into randomness; it could be about life, it could a critique of digital acceleration, it could be about time, about capitalism, about social media fatigue or something else. With this relentless barrage of shifting slides, it reminds me of some non-verbal critical documentaries like Koyaanisqatsi, but without a backbone.

The production is big, the music is good, yet it doesn't speak to me at all. Funny enough, he cast included an Iranian performer who occasionally speaks in Farsi, and sings a snippet of traditional Persian music. Even in these moments, in my own mother tongue, it feels so random, that doesn't speak to me.

DAY 8

The 8th day of the program, began with a short boat ride across Lake of Zurich. Then a practice sharing and a reflection session. Though we are exhausted, we are sad about the fast approaching end!

The 14th performance is a lecture performance called Vertiginous story of Orthosia by Jana Hadjithomas and Khalil Joreige. This day is the hottest day of our entire stay, in the theatre, I feel I cannot breathe anymore. Heat and the accumulated fatigue, push me to my limit; things do not enter my brain properly, I struggle to focus, see, read and listen.

Yet, I find this lecture fascinating: In the Northern Lebanon is the refugee camp Nahr el-Bared, home to Palestinians that were displaced in 1948. When the camp war partially destroyed in 2007, traces of an ancient Roman city destroyed by a tsunami in 551 BC were discovered, a city called Orthosia. The artist dig deep into the historical and the geopolitical layers of this discovery, interrogating the complex question and hidden narratives of political responsibilities toward displaced people and history itself. Among all the performances on the topic of war in this festival, this piece, stands out holding onto the most antiwar statement, where the complexity is addressed as complexity, where the researchers look for the complexity of war in a solid ground (literally), not for translating it to the international audience.

For the next performance we return to the floating stage to watch St. art by Cirk La Putyka. A cool evening breeze coming from the waters, sweeps away some of my frustration. I can finally breathe. The piece showcases acrobats and athletes occasionally touching on injuries and difficulties of athletes. Some stories about Olympics. And towards the end, it reveals a career ending injury that prevents one of the performers to throw the javelin on a target, instead, their dad is there to do it. A thin dry layer of clay covering the target, gradually clears off to show a phrase: No Limits. Why everything feels kind of cringe? The superficial approach and expectation on the body that is being criticized, is reproduced again by the performance. Theare of clearly Limits, there are injuries and there is failure, but the performance decides to swallow them and finish in a commercial-like advertisement in which the performers cosplay almost all the existing sports while climb a bouldering wall.

(This writing is getting so long that I regret starting it. But I have promised to finish it:

A very eye-catching installation in the center of the festival site, is a billboard displaying date and five different sentences, five promises, from news headlines, weather forecast, public statement and a personal promise. Nearby it, there is a station where participants can make a promise, swear an oath on an object (a rock, an apple, a Bible, a Quran and etc.), then have that promise written on the billboard for ten minutes.

I also make a promise. I thought about what is should be for days. What bothers me most right now (among things that I can change!), is the habit of jumping from something to another, without finishing them. So I promise this: I will promise to practice endings. starting from this text!)

DAY 9

Second to last day, began with a reflection session followed by an artist talk with Joana Hadjithomas. She is a remarkably inspiring researcher, deeply curious, taking the time to ask each of us about our own artistic practices while expanding on Orthosia and her other projects.

The first piece of the day, the 16th show overall is called: Ceramic Circus by Julian Vogel. For most of the performance, the word ceramic, is replaced with mosaic in my mind; matching very well with what I see: a solo circus where the performer puts various fragments of circus together. While doing so, He constantly balances on the verge of failure and pulls back just in time. As the audience, we thrill on the proximity of fall, we engage with the edgy moments near catastrophe.

In the grand finale, Julian attempts to to keep seven ceramic plates spinning simultaneously: ceramics – at this moment I realized that I made up the mosaic circus in my head. (which left me wondering why he had been roller-skating and cycling earlier in the piece?) Anyway, the attempt is doomed to fail, but he tries again and again, breaking all the plates. Even if I couldn't fully articulate why, I enjoy it. May be something about the beauty of aiming the impossible that I like. And embracing the failure.

The last show of the day is Corazon Rudo (Rough Heart) by Diana Rojas-Feile. A work about the Colombian civil war, drug trade and recruitment of children by illegal armed groups.

Before entering the theatre, we are instructed to store our bags and jackets in a locker, get headphones, and take our shoes off. The are informed that we can move freely in the space, the content is disturbing, any time the narrative gets overwhelming we can take our headphones off. As we enter the stage, there is a low eerie music playing. The space is lit in a dim blue color light. Large projection screens on two sides. Most of the content is delivered through a skillfully crafted video document, with live musicians performing behind one of the screens. Rojas-Feile approached the theatre primarily as a venue for a collective listening session. The work is very informative, very technical, but not so performative. Basically we all sit and listen.

DAY 10

It is finally here. Day ten!

At first we meet with the curators for a little exchange about watch and talk residency and the general program of the festival. Afterward, while some of us attended the awards ceremony, I skipped it to unwind and reflect in the beautiful afternoon colored with much life buzzing across the festival grounds.

The eighteenth and final performance of my ten-day marathon Pizza or a door in the dark does not dance by the collective Nature Theater of Oklahoma. A hilarious absurd musical that happens while waiting for Pizza and escalates when the pizza arrives. Full of references it shifts across Dadaistic, Surrealistic and absurdist genres yet always manages to land back on raw, essential human questions in life.

That's it. We watched it all.

This text me fourteen days to write. If you have read this far, I am deeply appreciate you. If you like to support my writing you can, ask me how. Or if you want to support mentally just tell me that you read it, what you though and what you felt. Careless Critique is my ongoing side project and would nourish with your feedback.

Of course, so much is unsaid, and I just passed real quick through the last works, but how can a person grasp everything in words? We are just here to try.

Despite my ongoing love-hate relationship with the theatre, I finish with this beautiful image that will hold me together a bit longer: a rainbow, coming out of the stage. A metaphor, for all the things that I want to say.

14 SEPTEMBER 2026

Den Haag
